ALIEN ESCAPE (originally "Tile Sprinter") - CHANGES FROM THE ONE-PAGER
Ryan Thleiji

THEME

- The one-pager had no story. Now, you play as an alien escaping
  Area 51, running across the desert while dodging traps left behind to slow you
  down. The title changed from "Tile Sprinter" to "Alien Escape" to match. The tiles
  fit the theme as a mix of traps (speed-up, falling boxes) and things that help
  you escape (gems, shields, jump pads).

CUT OR CHANGED

- Alternate movement modes (jetpack, etc.): Cut. The one-pager listed these as the
  least planned part of the design, and building separate physics, art, and sound
  for each mode was going to be a challenge. The jump pads (below) took over the
  role of "different movement" by launching the player much higher than a normal jump.

- Powerups: Merged into the tile system instead of being a separate pickup system.
  The shield comes from a blue tile. The "extra life" idea was dropped because the
  shield already covers it (it absorbs one hit).

- Crouch became a slide. The character art only has a single duck pose, which looked
  like sliding anyway, so it's presented as a slide with sand dust kicking up behind
  the player. Sliding shrinks the hitbox so the player fits under overhead obstacles.

TILES (as built)

- Gem (good): +50 points. Floats at body height, so running into it collects it and
  jumping skips it. Has a sparkle effect.
- Blue button - Shield (good): blocks the next obstacle you hit, shown as a bubble.
- Green button - Speed Up (bad): the game runs 1.5x faster for 4 seconds.
- Red button - Drop (bad): two boxes fall from the sky ahead of the player.

ADDED

- Jump pads and bait pads: This is how the one-pager's "tiles you are sometimes
  required to hit" works. A real jump pad is followed by a wall too tall to clear
  with a normal jump, so you must hit the pad. A bait pad sits under a hanging
  obstacle, so hitting it launches you into the obstacle. Real and bait pads look
  identical on purpose: the player decides whether to hit the pad by reading the
  obstacle that follows, not the pad's color.

- Fast fall: holding down in midair makes the player drop quickly. It's disabled
  during a jump pad launch so a bait pad can't be cancelled.

- How to Play page: press H on the start screen to see the controls and what each
  tile does, with the actual tile art next to each description.

- Speed lock: while a jump pad pattern is on screen, the game speed is held steady
  (a speed boost waits or keeps going until the pattern passes). Without this, a
  boost starting or ending mid-pattern threw off the launch timing.

- Difficulty ramp: the scroll speed increases over time up to a maximum, and
  obstacles come slightly closer together, but never closer than a safe minimum gap.

ART AND SOUND

- Art: Kenney "Platformer Pack Remastered" (green alien player with run, jump, and
  duck sprites; crates; sand ground; desert background; switches and gem for tiles).
  The ground and background scroll, with the background moving slower for depth.
- Sound: Kenney "Digital Audio" and "Impact Sounds" packs. Every action has its own
  sound (jump, land, slide, launch, each tile, shield pickup and break, death, game
  start). No background music, per the assignment guidelines.
- All Kenney assets are public domain (CC0).
